How Do We Grind a Stump on a Tight Lot?

Metairie lots are close together, and that changes how a stump job gets planned before it ever changes how it gets worked. Most properties here run narrow, with the house set close to one fence line and a driveway or gate taking up the other side. There often isn't a wide-open path from the street to the stump, so before we bring equipment in we walk the yard with you and figure out the route: which gate it fits through, what has to be protected along the way, where the neighbor's fence or shed sits relative to the stump itself.

That matters because a stump grinder throws wood chips and works close to whatever surrounds it. On a Metairie lot that usually means a fence a few feet away, sometimes on both sides, a sprinkler line running under the grass, or a slab or driveway edge right next to where the stump sits. We locate what's nearby before the grinder starts, not after something gets nicked. The goal on every job here is the same: take the stump out and leave everything around it exactly as it was.

We grind below grade, not just flush with the surface. On a New Orleans lot with room to spare, a stump ground level might be fine to leave alone for a while. On a tight Metairie yard, that ground usually gets used again fast: new sod, a paver patio, a fence replacement, an addition. A stump ground only to grade will still have wood underneath it, and that wood keeps breaking down. It settles unevenly under a lawn, it can shift a paver base, and it will absolutely cause problems under a poured slab or driveway. Grinding it down below grade and filling the hole level means whatever goes over that spot next, whether it's sod this month or concrete next year, sits on solid, settled ground instead of a stump that's still rotting six inches under it.

Leaving a stump in place on a lot this size costs more than it looks like it saves. It's a mower hazard and a trip hazard in a yard that's already tight on usable space. It sprouts new growth from the roots if it isn't treated. It holds water on Metairie's flat, low-lying ground, which means standing water sitting closer to the house than you'd want. And it takes up room that, on a suburban lot, is worth using for something else. Grinding it now clears that ground for good, instead of working around it for years.

Stump Grinding FAQs

How close to my fence or my neighbor's yard can you grind a stump? +
Closer than most people expect. Our equipment is sized for tight Metairie lots, and we walk the property first to plan the approach around fences, AC units, sheds, and whatever sits on the other side of the line.
Why grind the stump instead of just leaving it? +
A stump left in the ground keeps rotting under the lawn, holds water, sprouts new growth, and settles unevenly if you ever pour a driveway, patio, or slab over it. Grinding it below grade now avoids all of that later.
Will grinding damage my driveway, slab, or sprinkler lines? +
That is exactly what the walk-through before the job is for. We locate irrigation lines, note what's nearby, and grind with that in mind so what stays, stays intact.
