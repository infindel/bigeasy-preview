The method

How Does Stump Grinding Work on the Northshore?

Stump grinding on the Northshore rarely means one stump in a backyard. St. Tammany and the surrounding parishes carry a lot more wooded acreage than the New Orleans side of the lake, and most of the stump work here follows something bigger: a tree removal that covered a half-acre lot, a mulching job that opened up several acres of pine and hardwood, or a land-clearing scope that took out a tree line for a new driveway or building pad. Where a New Orleans yard might have one stump to deal with, a Northshore tract can leave a dozen scattered across the ground, and the estimate has to account for that count and the distance between them, not just the width of a single stump.

Access shapes the job before the grinder even starts. Long gravel drives, gated tracts, and ground that stays soft after spring rain are normal out here, and getting a stump grinder to the back of a property is part of the plan, not an afterthought. On land still carrying stumps from an earlier clearing or mulching pass, we walk the whole tract first and mark what's coming out, so the crew isn't finding stumps mid-job that should have already been on the quote.

The work itself doesn't change with the address: every stump gets ground below grade, not just knocked flush with the surface. Flush is where the next problem starts. A stump cut level with the ground still has a root mass rotting underneath, and on Northshore ground that holds water longer after a storm, that rot moves faster and whatever sits above it settles unevenly. Below grade, backfilled and rough-graded, that spot is ready for whatever comes next: a driveway extension, a building pad, sod, or a gravel base that won't sink six months later.

Leaving stumps in the ground costs more the longer they sit. Sprouts push back up through mowed pasture and food plots for seasons. A stump near a septic field or a fence line keeps rotting where nobody can see it, and a mower or a footing eventually finds it the hard way. On land being turned into pasture, trails, or a mulching-friendly stand for hunting or grazing, stumps left in place are the difference between ground you can actually use and ground you're still working around years later. Whether it's three stumps left from a driveway clearing or a whole tract after a mulching pass, one estimate covers the full count.

Northshore Stump Grinding FAQs

Do you grind stumps on large wooded Northshore lots, or just a single yard stump? +
Both. A single backyard stump and a tract left with a dozen stumps after a clearing or mulching job are different scopes, and we walk the property first so the estimate covers the actual count, not a guess.
Why grind a stump below grade instead of just cutting it flush? +
A stump cut flush still has a root mass rotting underneath, and it keeps rotting. On Northshore ground that holds water longer after a storm, that decay moves faster and whatever sits above it settles unevenly. Below grade closes that out for good.
Can stump grinding run alongside a bigger land-clearing or mulching job out here? +
Yes. On acreage being cleared or mulched, grinding the stumps in the same visit means the ground is actually usable when the crew leaves, instead of covered in stumps you still have to schedule a second trip for.
