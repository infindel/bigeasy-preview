The Metairie approach

How Do We Handle Demolition on Tight Lots?

Metairie lots run tight. Most subdivisions here, whether it's the older grid off Causeway or the newer streets past Bonnabel, put houses close enough together that a demolition job has two fence lines to think about, not one. Before we touch a shed, a carport, or an old slab, we walk the property with you and map out access: how equipment gets in, where debris stages, and what stays untouched. The patio you're keeping. The sprinkler line. The neighbor's fence a few feet from the wall coming down. That planning matters more here than out on open acreage, where there's room to work around a mistake. On a standard Metairie lot, there usually isn't, so the walk-through happens before anything gets touched, not after.

The work itself covers structures that have outlived their purpose. A shed that's rotted through or leaning. An old carport nobody uses anymore. A slab left behind from a pool house or garage that came down years ago and never got cleared. We take the structure down, break up and remove the slab if there is one, and haul everything off the property. What's left is clean ground, ready for a driveway extension, a new build, or just grass again. On lots close to the canals or older drainage easements, we also check how runoff moves across the site before we start, since a slab sitting flush with the ground can be doing more for drainage than it looks like.

Do I Need a Permit to Demolish a Structure in Metairie?

One thing worth knowing before you call anyone for this kind of job: structure demolition in Jefferson Parish can require permits, depending on what's coming down and where the property sits. We are not attorneys and we do not pull permits. If your project needs one, confirming that requirement with the parish is on you or your contractor before work starts. We'll tell you plainly if a job looks like it needs paperwork, but we won't guess on your behalf, and we won't start work that should have paperwork attached to it first.

Metairie's older neighborhoods also mean older structures. A slab poured decades ago doesn't always match what's on record, and a shed that looks simple from the street sometimes sits on a foundation that changes the job. That's why every quote starts with us looking at the actual site instead of a description over the phone. Free, fast, and accurate means the number reflects what's really there, not a guess made from a photo or an address.
