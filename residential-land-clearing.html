How it works

What Does a Residential Job Look Like?

Every residential job starts with a walk of the property, not a guess from a photo. We look at what's actually there: the trees that need to come down, the brush that needs clearing, the stumps left behind, and whether the ground needs grading before anything else can happen on it. Working close to a house, a fence, or a neighbor's yard takes a different plan than clearing open acreage, and we tell you what that plan is before we start.

From there, one crew runs the whole job. Tree removal, brush clearing, stump grinding, grading and site leveling, and debris hauling all come from the same team, so you aren't coordinating separate contractors for each piece of a backyard project.

Getting Ready to Build on Your Lot?

A garage, an addition, or a new outbuilding all start the same way: the footprint has to be clear, and the ground under it has to be stable. We clear the trees and brush, grind the stumps, and grade the site so your builder isn't working around roots and ruts. If you're planning ahead, our guide on preparing a lot for a home build walks through what that looks like before a crew ever shows up.

After a storm, the priority shifts to what came down. Our guide on storm-damaged tree removal covers what to check for and when a downed or leaning tree needs a crew right away.

Residential Land Clearing FAQs

What counts as residential land clearing? +
It covers backyard reclamation, hazard tree removal near a house or fence line, clearing a footprint for a garage or addition, and storm debris cleanup. Whatever the yard needs, one crew handles tree removal, brush clearing, stump grinding, grading, and hauling as part of the same job.
Can you remove a hazard tree without damaging my yard or house? +
That is the point of walking the property before anything starts. We plan the drop zone, work around fences and structures nearby, and clear the debris once the tree is down. Every yard is different, which is why the estimate comes after we have actually seen the tree.
Do you clear storm debris after a hurricane or major storm? +
Yes. Downed limbs, snapped trunks, and storm debris on a residential lot are common calls across New Orleans, Metairie, Baton Rouge, and the Northshore. A crew comes out, clears what came down, and hauls it off.
