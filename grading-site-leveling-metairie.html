Grading a Metairie Lot Is a Different Job

Metairie lots sit close together. A fence on the left, a driveway on the right, a patio or shed that has to stay exactly where it is. That changes how a grading job gets planned before it changes how it gets done. We walk the property first and mark what stays: the fence lines, the AC unit, the mature tree, the neighbor's flower bed six feet off the property line. Equipment gets sized to the access, not the other way around, and a narrow side yard often means smaller machinery running a slower, more careful pass instead of one big push across the lot.

The ground itself is the other half of the job. Metairie sits flat and low, and flat, low ground does not shed water on its own. A yard that looks fine in dry weather can hold water for days after a real storm, and the spot closest to the slab is usually the one that never dries. Fixing that means more than dumping fill in the low corner. We cut grade so water actually has somewhere to go, with fall running away from the foundation instead of pooling against it, and we tie the new grade into what the lot is already doing so the fix holds instead of just moving the puddle ten feet.

Pad prep works the same way. An addition, a garage slab, a shed base, all need a level, compacted surface before anyone builds on it, and on a tight lot that pad has to sit right without crowding what's next to it. Grading and demolition often show up on the same job too, when an old slab or driveway has to come out before the new grade goes in. We handle it as one job: level the pad, correct the drainage, and clean up the low ground, all under one estimate and one crew.
