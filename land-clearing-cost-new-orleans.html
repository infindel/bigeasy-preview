How Much Does Land Clearing Cost in New Orleans?

Quick answer

Land clearing cost comes down to lot size, how dense the growth is, how the crew can get equipment onto the property, and what has to be hauled off versus mulched in place. Stumps and grading, if they're part of the job, add their own scope. Every parcel in southeast Louisiana is a little different, and that's exactly why a real land clearing estimate can't be pulled out of thin air. Two lots the same size a mile apart can carry very different scopes of work once you look at what's actually growing on them and how a crew gets equipment onto the property. A property in Metairie behaves nothing like acreage out toward Baton Rouge or a wooded parcel on the Northshore, even when the paperwork lists similar square footage. Here's what we're actually weighing when we walk your land, and why we'd rather show up and look than guess from a description.

How Much Does Lot Size Really Matter?

Size sets a baseline, but it's not the whole story. A half-acre choked with vine, palmetto, and overgrown brush can take a crew longer than two open acres of scattered pine. Shape matters too: a long, narrow lot with an odd angle takes more maneuvering than a square parcel of the same total area. That's why we don't quote off a plat map or a satellite photo alone. If you want a general sense of how acreage plays into a job, our guide to clearing one acre in Louisiana walks through it in more detail.

Does Growth Density Change the Job More Than Acreage Does?

Often, yes. Thick understory and tangled vines slow a machine down, and dense palmetto adds even more drag, no matter how many acres are on the deed. Open pasture with a few scattered trees moves fast. Wooded ground that hasn't been touched in years moves slower, acre for acre, and that difference shows up in the estimate. The size and species of the trees themselves matter as well: scattered pines clear differently than a stand of mature hardwoods, and that changes both the equipment we bring and the time on site.

How Much Does Site Access Affect the Price?

A tight urban lot near a fence line and a gate is a different job than open rural acreage on the Northshore. Narrow access, soft or wet ground, and distance from the nearest road all change how equipment gets in and how long setup takes. A single-entry driveway or a shared easement can mean extra time getting machinery positioned before any actual clearing starts. We look at approach and terrain on the same walk we look at the trees, because a beautiful stand of brush behind a gate too narrow for our equipment is its own kind of problem.

What's the Difference Between Hauling and Mulching, Cost-Wise?

Forestry mulching processes brush and small trees in place with one machine, no burn piles, no trucks hauling material off. Traditional clearing digs vegetation out and hauls it away, which adds trucking and disposal to the job. Neither approach is automatically cheaper. The right one depends on the land, what you want it to look like when the crew leaves, and whether you're headed toward full land clearing in New Orleans or just want the overgrowth knocked back.

  • Lot size and shape
  • How dense the brush, vines, and undergrowth are
  • Whether equipment can reach the work area easily
  • Whether material is hauled off-site or mulched in place
  • Whether stump grinding is part of the scope
  • Whether the land needs grading or site leveling once it's clear

Most properties need a mix of these rather than just one. That's the advantage of a crew that handles tree removal, brush clearing, stump grinding, and grading under one roof: we walk the whole property, tell you plainly which approach fits which section, and roll it into a single estimate instead of sending you to three different contractors.

Do Stumps and Grading Add to the Estimate?

They can, if they're part of what you need. Stump grinding is its own line item because it's its own pass with different equipment, and the number of stumps and their size both factor in. Grading and site leveling matter most when the land is headed toward a slab, a driveway, or anything that needs a flat, compacted surface. If you just want the lot cleared and left as-is, that scope stays out of the number entirely. We ask what the land is for before we quote, because that answer changes what actually needs doing, and there's no reason to pay for grading work you don't need.

Why Is a Walk-the-Land Estimate More Accurate Than a Guess Over the Phone?

Photos and a phone description get you a ballpark. They don't get you an accurate number. Density and access are hard to judge from a picture, and ground condition is harder still. That's why our estimate is free: we'd rather send someone to look than hand you a figure we might have to change later. Free, fast, and accurate isn't a slogan we picked because it sounds nice. It means the number you hear after we walk your property is the number you pay, full stop.
