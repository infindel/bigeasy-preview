The method

How Does Tree Removal Work on the Northshore?

Northshore tree work rarely means one tree in a small yard. Parishes like St. Tammany carry a lot more wooded acreage than the New Orleans side of the lake, and properties here run from a single wooded lot to tracts of pine and hardwood that stretch well past what you can see from the road. That changes how a job gets planned. Before anything comes down, we walk the property with you, look at what's standing, and give a free, fast, accurate estimate that accounts for the actual scope, not a flat per-tree number that ignores how many acres are involved.

Access is its own factor out here. A lot of Northshore properties sit down long gravel drives, behind gates, or off roads that don't leave much room to stage equipment. We plan the approach before the saws start, so getting a bucket truck or mulcher to the back of the tract doesn't turn into its own project. On acreage with a heavier tree count, we also look at whether forestry mulching makes more sense alongside straight removal. Mulching can clear brush and smaller growth in place while we take down the larger trees that actually need to come out, which keeps a big job from turning into a slow one.

The trees themselves tell you what kind of day it's going to be. Loblolly and longleaf pine stands are common on Northshore ground, and pines fail differently than a live oak does: they snap high, they lean under their own weight after a wet season, and a dead pine can hold a lot of tension that isn't obvious from the ground. Storm-damaged trees get looked at first, whether that's a pine already down across a driveway or an oak that's leaning but hasn't gone over yet. A tree in that condition next to a home, a barn, or an access road is a hazard, and we treat it like one.

Once a tree is down, the job isn't done until the wood and debris are gone and the stump is handled if you want it gone too. On rural and larger properties that often means hauling more debris off the tract than a typical in-town yard would produce, and we plan the estimate around that reality instead of surprising you with it later. The goal on every Northshore job is the same one we use everywhere: leave the ground usable, whether that's a cleared building pad, an open driveway, or acreage that finally looks like the plan you had for it.

Northshore Tree Removal FAQs

Can you remove trees on wooded Northshore acreage, not just a single yard tree? +
Yes. Northshore properties often run from a half-acre to many acres of wooded ground, and we quote both ends the same way: walk the tract, see what's standing, and give you a free, fast, accurate number before anything gets cut.
What do you do with storm-damaged trees after high winds? +
We take down what the storm already weakened, whether it's still standing at a lean, hung up in another tree, or split and holding by a thread. That work gets prioritized because a compromised tree near a house or driveway is a hazard, not a project to schedule around.
Do you handle the stump and the debris, or just the tree? +
Both, if you want them. Stump grinding and debris hauling are part of the service, and on larger wooded lots we can run forestry mulching alongside the removal so the cleared ground is usable right away.
