What Does Lot Preparation Involve?

Clearing & grubbing

Trees, stumps, brush, and root mass come out down to grade so nothing is left to settle or rot under a slab.

Rough grading

Cut and fill work that establishes the pad's general shape and moves the bulk of the dirt.

Finish grading

The fine pass that brings the surface to the exact elevation your builder or engineer called for.

Drainage-aware grading

Every pass accounts for where the water goes once it rains, because in this parish it always rains.

The method

How Do You Get a Lot to Build-Ready?

We start with a walk of the property and the paperwork that matters: the builder's site plan, the elevation certificate if there is one, and whatever your engineer has specified for the pad. That number drives everything after it.

Clearing comes first, taking the footprint down to bare ground and pulling stumps and roots rather than grinding over them. Rough grading follows, cutting high spots and filling low ones until the pad holds its general shape. Finish grading is the last pass: compacting and fine-tuning the surface until it sits at the elevation you were quoted, with a grade that carries water away from where the structure will sit instead of toward it.

Lot Prep, Grading, or Both?

Most build sites need the full sequence, not one piece of it. Standing trees mean a tree removal scope before a machine ever grades. A pad that's already clear but sitting at the wrong elevation is straight grading and site leveling. One crew running both means one estimate for the whole job instead of two contractors handing off and pointing fingers when the grade doesn't match. Near wetlands or drainage-sensitive ground, we bring the same care as our environmental services work and say plainly what's workable before equipment moves in.

Lot Preparation FAQs

What does lot preparation include? +
Clearing and grubbing to remove trees, stumps, and root mass, followed by rough grading to shape the pad and finish grading to bring it to the exact elevation your builder or engineer specified. We grade with drainage in mind at every stage, not as an afterthought.
How is lot preparation different from land clearing? +
Clearing removes what is growing on the land. Lot preparation goes further: it takes cleared ground and shapes it into a pad that sits at the correct elevation and sheds water the way it should. A parcel headed for a slab needs both, usually in the same scope.
What is a builder elevation, and why does it matter? +
It is the pad height your builder, engineer, or flood elevation certificate calls for. Grade too low and you build in a bathtub. Grade too high and fill costs climb and drainage gets harder to manage. We work to the number you give us and confirm it before the finish pass.
Do you handle drainage during lot preparation? +
Yes. Southeast Louisiana does not forgive a flat pad or a low corner. We grade to move water off the lot and away from where the structure will sit, and on wetland-adjacent parcels we bring the same approach we use for our environmental services work.
