The method

How Do We Haul Debris Off Tight Metairie Lots?

Metairie lots don't give a hauling job room to breathe. Houses sit close on both sides, driveways run narrow, and there's usually a fence, a flower bed, or an AC unit within a few feet of wherever a truck needs to sit. Before anything gets loaded, we walk the property and work out the access: which gate the material moves through, where the truck can actually park without blocking a neighbor's driveway, and what has to be covered or fenced off so it comes through the job untouched.

What comes off a typical Metairie lot is rarely just one thing. A side yard cleared of overgrown brush, a stack of trunk sections left after a tree removal, plus old fence boards and deck lumber from a backyard project: it can all go out in the same load instead of three separate trips. We sort it and load it, then haul it completely off the property. The job isn't done when the pile gets stacked at the curb waiting on pickup. It's done when the truck pulls away and the lot is actually clear.

Working between two occupied houses changes how the crew moves. Equipment stays on tarps or plywood where the ground could rut, the work area stays contained instead of spreading across the whole yard, and anything staying put, the good trees, the sprinkler heads, the driveway itself, gets protected before the first load goes on the truck. A hauling job that leaves ruts in the lawn or a cracked paver on the way out isn't finished, it just traded one mess for another.

Most debris hauling calls in Metairie come out of something bigger: a lot getting cleared ahead of an addition, storm debris after wind and heavy rain move through the parish, or a demo job that produced more material than a homeowner's truck can move in one trip. We handle the hauling as part of that larger scope when there is one, or take it on as a standalone job when the debris is the only thing that needs to go. Either way, the estimate is built around what's actually sitting on your property, not a guess made over the phone, and it's free, fast, and accurate before a single load gets hauled.

Metairie Debris Hauling FAQs

Can you haul debris from a tight side yard with no truck access? +
Yes. Most Metairie lots don't give a truck a straight shot to the pile, so we plan the route in first: which gate the material moves through, where the truck actually sits, and what gets protected along the way. Narrow access changes the plan, not whether the job gets done.
Will there be a pile left at the curb when you're done? +
No. The debris leaves the property completely, brush, trunks, and demo material loaded and hauled off in the same visit. Nothing gets staged at the curb to wait on a second pickup.
Do you haul debris that isn't from a job you did? +
Yes. Standalone hauling is common on tight lots: storm debris, material another crew left behind, or a brush pile that's been sitting since a side-yard cleanup. You don't need to have hired us for the original work.
