Grading & Site Leveling in New Orleans, LA

Quick answer

Grading and site leveling reshapes a lot so water moves off it instead of pooling against your house, shed, or slab. On flat southeast Louisiana ground that means fall away from structures, filled low spots, and a compacted, buildable pad. When Do You Need Grading and Site Leveling?

Standing water after every rain

A low spot near the house, the shed, or the driveway that never dries out between storms.

A new slab or pad

Fall toward the house instead of away

The method

How Does Site Grading Work?

Grading starts with reading the lot: where water sits now, where it needs to go instead, and how far off level the ground actually is. From there we cut the high spots, fill the low ones, and shape a consistent slope that carries water away from the house, the garage, or wherever you're about to build. On a lot headed for a slab, that grade gets compacted in stages so the pad holds weight instead of settling under it later.

Louisiana ground makes this trickier than it looks. Heavy clay holds water instead of draining it, and a summer downpour can turn a barely-off-grade yard into a standing pond by the back door. We work the fall away from structures first, because a foundation with water sitting against it for years is a much bigger repair than a yard that's a few inches off level. Wet or soft sections get handled the same drainage-aware way we approach environmental services work, worked out before any heavy equipment rolls.

Grading & Site Leveling FAQs

How much does grading and site leveling cost in New Orleans? +
It depends on the size of the area, how far off level the ground is, and whether it's headed for a lawn, a driveway, or a slab. Because every lot drains differently, we walk the property before quoting it. The estimate is free, fast, and accurate, so you get a real number before anything is scheduled.
Why does the grade around my house matter? +
Water follows the slope of the ground, and if that slope tilts toward your foundation instead of away from it, water sits against the house every time it rains. Over time that's a much bigger repair than a regrade. Fixing the fall away from structures is usually the first thing we look at on a site visit.
Can grading fix a low spot that always holds water? +
In most cases, yes. Low spots get filled and shaped so water has somewhere to go instead of pooling in place. On sites with more serious drainage or wetland-adjacent conditions, we handle it the same careful way we approach our environmental services work.
