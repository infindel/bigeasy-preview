Quick answer

Environmental services at Big Easy Land Clearing means land work done with drainage, wetlands, plus waterways in mind: equipment sized to the ground, fewer passes on soft soil, and buffers respected around ditches, canals, plus marsh edges. We are not a permitting authority. Wetland determinations belong to the Corps of Engineers and state and parish agencies, and we tell you plainly what we can see before any machine rolls. Read the ground first

What Makes a Property Environmentally Sensitive?

Standing water & soft ground

Low spots and seasonal flooding call for tracked equipment and fewer passes, not the heaviest machine on the lot.

Ditches, canals & bayou frontage

We keep buffers around waterways and drainage paths so runoff and erosion aren't the price of a cleared parcel.

Wetland-adjacent parcels

If your property sits near a marked or suspected wetland, a formal determination comes from the Corps of Engineers or the state, not from us.

Protected trees & buffers

Some trees and buffer zones fall under parish or local ordinance. We flag what we notice so you can check before anything comes down.

The approach

How Does Environmental Services Work?

We start with a walk of the property, not a truck full of equipment. On wetland-adjacent or drainage-sensitive ground, that walk matters more than the estimate itself. We look at where water sits after rain, where it drains to, how close the tree line runs to a ditch or canal, and how soft the ground gets underfoot. That tells us which machine fits the job and which parts of the parcel need a lighter touch.

Forestry mulching often does the heavy lifting here. One tracked machine makes fewer passes than an excavator-and-truck operation, grinds material where it stands, and leaves a natural mulch layer that holds soil in place instead of stripping it bare. Where the ground calls for something else, we say so before we start. We never treat a guess about a wetland boundary as a fact. If a parcel needs an official determination, that call belongs to the Corps of Engineers or state and parish agencies, and we point you toward the right office rather than working around the question.

Environmental Services, or Something Else?

Most environmentally sensitive jobs still need standard clearing work somewhere on the same property. A dry building pad can sit right next to a wet, low corner that needs a different approach entirely. That's the case for one crew handling the whole scope: we walk the land with you, mark which sections call for a lighter method, and put it all on one estimate. Forestry mulching usually fits the sensitive ground, and grading and site leveling picks up once the drainage-aware work is done and the buildable area is defined.

Not sure which side of that line your property falls on? Start with a free walk of the land. We'll tell you what we see, what's workable, and what belongs to an agency to decide.

Environmental Services FAQs

Do you handle wetland permits or determinations? +
No. We are not a permitting authority and we don't issue wetland determinations. That call belongs to the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and Louisiana state and parish agencies. We walk your property, tell you plainly what we observe, and match our method to it. If your project needs an official determination, we point you to the office that makes it.
How do you clear land near a wetland or canal? +
Usually with forestry mulching. One tracked machine makes fewer passes than an excavator-and-truck operation, grinds material in place, and keeps buffers around ditches, canals, and marsh edges intact. We tell you what's workable on your specific ground before any equipment moves.
What if my property has standing water or soft ground? +
That changes the equipment and the plan, not whether the job can get done. Low spots and soft soil call for lighter, tracked machines and fewer passes across the ground. We look at the site on the estimate visit and tell you straight what fits.
Where do you provide environmental services? +
New Orleans, Metairie, Baton Rouge, the Northshore, and surrounding southeast Louisiana communities.
