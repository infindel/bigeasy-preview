How Do We Handle Northshore Debris?

Debris hauling on the Northshore looks different than it does on a quarter-acre city lot. Properties here run bigger: half-acre, one-acre, sometimes several wooded acres in Covington, Mandeville, Slidell, or out toward Hammond. When we clear a tract like that, whether it's for a new build, a bigger yard, or just thinning overgrown timber, the volume of material left behind is real. Trunks, limbs, root balls, and brush pile up fast across an acre in a way they never would on a small city lot, and that pile has to leave the property before the job is actually done.

Access matters too. A lot of Northshore work sits off gravel drives, long private roads, or land without a paved street in front of it. There's no curb to stack debris at and no municipal truck coming by later to take it away. In the unincorporated parts of St. Tammany and the surrounding parishes, there isn't a bulk pickup service to catch what a land-clearing crew leaves behind. If we don't haul it, it sits there, and a cleared lot with a pile of brush at the tree line isn't a cleared lot.

Forestry mulching handles a lot of what grows on Northshore acreage: underbrush, small saplings, vines, the tangle that makes a wooded tract hard to walk. But mulching has limits. Bigger trunks, thick root balls, and anything that came down from a full tree removal don't grind down small enough to leave in place, and a property owner who wants the land actually usable, not just mulched over, needs that material trucked out. Same story with demolition work: old sheds, fencing, storm-wrecked structures. Debris hauling is what finishes those jobs, not an extra add-on tacked on afterward.

On a typical Northshore job, hauling covers whatever the clearing or demo work produces: brush and limbs from mulching or brush clearing, trunks and stumps from tree removal, and demo material from a torn-down structure. It all comes off the property in the same trip our equipment is already there for, which keeps the job to one estimate and one crew instead of calling a separate hauler after the fact. The property gets handed back clear, whether that's a wooded lot ready for a foundation or a yard ready to mow, with nothing left behind at the tree line or stacked by the drive.

Debris Hauling FAQs

Do you haul debris from rural Northshore properties without curbside pickup? +
Yes. Unincorporated parts of the Northshore don't have municipal bulk pickup, so if the debris isn't hauled off by us, it stays on the property. Hauling is part of the job, not a separate call.
What kind of debris do you haul off wooded acreage? +
Brush, limbs, trunks, root balls, and stumps from clearing or tree removal, plus anything mulching can't grind down small enough to leave in place.
Do you haul demolition debris too? +
Yes. Old sheds, fencing, and storm-wrecked structures get hauled off the same way as land-clearing debris, as part of the same estimate.
