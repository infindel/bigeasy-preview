The method

How Does Forestry Mulching Work on a Tight Lot?

Metairie lots run tight. Most of the parish's subdivisions were built out from the 1950s through the 1970s, when a 60- or 70-foot lot with a fence on both sides was the standard, not the exception. That layout is what changes the job. A crew clearing acreage on the Northshore can walk equipment around a problem area with room to spare. A crew clearing a brushy side yard in Metairie is working between your fence and the neighbor's, often with a shed, an AC unit, or a tree root flare sitting right where the machine needs to pass. Access gets planned before the mulcher ever starts, not worked out on the fly. That planning conversation happens on the walkthrough, before any equipment leaves the trailer, so nobody's guessing where the machine can and can't go once the work is underway.

Forestry mulching fits this kind of lot better than most methods because it's one machine doing one job. The mulcher grinds brush, small trees, vines, and undergrowth into material right where it stands, instead of cutting, piling, and hauling in separate trips across a yard that barely has room for one truck. Fewer passes near the fence line means less risk to what you want kept: a fence, a shed, a tree you're not touching. The ground-up material stays down as a mulch layer instead of leaving bare dirt, which matters on the flat, often soggy ground common in this parish. Bare soil next to a drainage ditch or canal erodes and washes; a mulch layer holds.

We walk the lot with you first and mark what stays: fences, structures, trees you want kept, utility lines. Then one operator runs the mulcher in a single, controlled pass over the area coming out. No stacked debris pile waiting on a second visit, no separate haul-off crew tracking through the yard behind it. When the machine is done, the brush is gone and the ground is covered, not stripped. Same equipment as a rural clearing job, aimed at a smaller and more crowded target, with a plan for what has to be protected before the first cut.

Metairie Forestry Mulching FAQs

What is forestry mulching, and how is it different on a tight Metairie lot? +
Forestry mulching grinds brush, small trees, and undergrowth into mulch right where it stands, in one pass, instead of cutting and hauling in separate trips. On a Metairie lot with fences close on both sides, that means fewer passes near property lines and less equipment movement through a tight space.
Will mulching disturb my fence or my neighbor's yard? +
We walk the lot first and mark what stays: fences, structures, trees you're keeping. The machine works the marked area in a controlled pass. That planning step is what keeps the job inside your property line.
What happens to the cleared brush and undergrowth? +
It gets ground into mulch and left on the ground rather than hauled off. On the flat, often soggy ground common in this parish, that mulch layer holds the soil instead of leaving it bare.
