The method

How We Prep a Metairie Lot

Most Metairie lots are 50 to 60 feet wide with a house sitting a few feet off the property line on either side. That changes how the whole job gets planned. Before equipment ever shows up, we walk the lot and figure out access: which side has room for a skid steer, where material can stage without sitting against the neighbor's fence, and what has to be hand-cleared because there's no other way in. On a corner lot or a lot backing up to a canal or drainage ditch, that plan looks different again.

Clearing comes first, and on an infill lot that usually means less brush and more decision-making: a shed that has to come down, a section of chain-link that isn't part of the scope, a tree the owner wants kept for shade. We clear what's leaving and rope off what's staying before the first pass, because on a lot this size there's no buffer for a mistake.

Grading is where the real work happens. Metairie sits low and flat, and a lot of the older subdivisions were built before modern drainage standards, which means water doesn't always find its own way off the property. We rough-grade first to get the lot to shape, then finish-grade to the elevation your builder or engineer has set, whether that's a slab height, a pier-and-beam foundation, or a pad for a future build. Getting that elevation right the first time matters, because reworking a graded pad after forms are set costs everyone time. We also shape the grade so runoff moves toward the street or an existing drain instead of pooling against the house or running onto the neighbor's yard, which is the fastest way to turn a lot-prep job into a dispute with the people next door.

Pad leveling is the last step: a flat, compacted, build-ready surface handed off clean, with the debris hauled and the site walked one more time before we call it done. If the lot needs more than that, tree removal, stump grinding, or a heavier grading scope, it's the same crew and the same estimate, not a second company and a second quote.

Lot Preparation FAQs

What does lot preparation include in Metairie? +
Clearing whatever is on the lot, rough and finish grading, and pad leveling to the elevation your builder or plans call for. The goal is a clean, level, build-ready lot with the drainage worked out before the first form gets set.
Can you work on a tight lot with neighbors close on both sides? +
Yes. Most Metairie lots run narrow with houses close on either side. We plan access and equipment around that up front so fences, driveways, and whatever else is staying stays untouched.
Do you handle drainage and grading to the builder's elevation? +
Yes. We grade to the elevation your builder or engineer sets and shape the site so water moves off the lot and toward the street or drain, not toward the neighbors.
