The method

How Do We Prep a Northshore Lot?

Northshore lots start further from finished than a New Orleans in-town parcel. Many of the tracts we work are two, five, ten acres or more, still wooded, with a driveway cut in from a rural road and maybe a septic field to route around. Turning that into a build-ready pad is a different job than clearing a quarter-acre city lot, and it starts with a walk of the whole tract, not just the building footprint.

First pass is clearing: trees, brush, and undergrowth off the pad and anywhere the septic, driveway, or utility runs need to go. On a lot with scattered mid-size timber and heavy undergrowth, mulching in place often beats hauling everything off site. It clears the ground and works organic matter back into the soil instead of leaving a debris pile waiting on a truck. We size that call to the tract: dense hardwood stands usually come out and get hauled, while brushy secondary growth on a bigger footprint is often faster and cheaper mulched.

Once the ground is clear, grading starts. Rough grading cuts and fills to get the pad roughly level and pulls out stumps, roots, and buried debris the clearing pass missed. Finish grading brings the pad to the elevation your builder specified, because on Northshore ground that number matters. Much of this area sits low and flat, water tables run high, and a pad set even a few inches off can mean standing water against a foundation after the first hard rain. We grade with drainage in mind from the start: a slight fall away from the building envelope and drainage paths that don't dead-end at a fence line or a neighbor's low spot.

Rural access shapes the job too. A lot with a narrow gravel drive or a gated entrance limits what equipment can get back to the pad, and we plan the machinery around that instead of finding out on site. Bigger acreage means bigger clearing scopes: more debris volume, more grading yardage, sometimes staged work if the tract is being developed in phases.

The goal is the same every time: raw land handed off ready for the next trade, whether that's a foundation crew, a septic installer, or a driveway contractor. One estimate, one crew, and a pad that's actually ready when they show up.

It depends on the acreage, how wooded the tract is, and how much grading the pad needs. That's why the estimate is free, fast, and accurate: a real number after we walk the land, not a guess over the phone.

Yes. Northshore lots run wooded and rural, often with a narrow drive or a gated entrance. We size the equipment to the tract and the access that has to carry it in.
