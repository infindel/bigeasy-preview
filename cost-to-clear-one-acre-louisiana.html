How Much Does It Cost to Clear 1 Acre of Land in Louisiana?

Quick answer

There's no single per-acre price because what's on the acre drives the job, not the acre itself. Dense pine costs more to clear than scattered brush, wet ground calls for different equipment than dry ground, and mulching versus clear-and-haul changes the scope entirely. Ask five landowners what it cost to clear an acre in Louisiana and you'll get five different answers, and none of them will help you figure out your own number. That's not because pricing is a mystery. It's because "an acre" tells you almost nothing about the job. Two acres side by side can call for completely different equipment, completely different time on site, and completely different scope, because what's on the acre is the real variable, not the acreage itself.

What Actually Drives the Cost to Clear an Acre?

Every clearing job comes down to the same handful of variables, and they matter more than the acreage on the deed:

  • What's standing on the land: scattered brush, dense pine, hardwood, or a mix
  • Trunk size, and how much of it needs full removal versus mulching down
  • Ground condition: dry and firm, or soft, low, and wet
  • Access, meaning whether equipment can get in easily or a path has to be cut first
  • What you want done with the material: mulched in place or hauled off the property
  • What the finished ground needs to be ready for, whether that's grazing, a build pad, or just walkable again

Change any one of those and the job changes with it. That's the honest reason per-acre pricing swings the way it does, and it's the same reason we don't quote a number over the phone before we've seen the ground.

Does Dense Pine Cost More Than Scattered Brush?

Usually, yes, and it's not close. An acre of scattered brush and saplings might take a fraction of the machine time that a stand of dense pine or mixed hardwood needs. More trunks means more cuts, more material to process, and more time the equipment spends on that acre instead of moving to the next one. Diameter matters too. A parcel thick with small-diameter pine mulches down fast. A parcel with a few large, mature trees mixed into the brush usually needs those trees removed separately before mulching finishes the rest. That's why we walk the land before we price it: brush density and tree size on your specific acre are two of the biggest swings in the whole estimate.

Why Does Wet Ground Change the Price?

South Louisiana ground doesn't behave like ground anywhere else, and wet or soft soil changes what equipment can safely do the job. Low-lying, saturated, or seasonally wet acreage often calls for tracked machines that spread weight evenly and make fewer passes, rather than heavier equipment that would rut the ground or get stuck outright. Fewer passes and the right machine for the soil both affect time on site, and time on site is a real part of what you're paying for. This is also where forestry mulching tends to pull ahead of traditional clear-and-haul: it typically means less equipment moving across sensitive ground. Our forestry mulching vs. traditional land clearing guide breaks down that comparison in more detail.

Is Mulching Cheaper Than Clear-and-Haul?

It depends on what the finished acre needs to look like. Mulching grinds brush and small-to-medium trees in place and leaves a natural mulch layer behind, so there's no haul-off truck traffic and often fewer passes across the land. Clear-and-haul physically removes material from the property, which adds trucking and disposal into the job, but it's the right call when the ground needs to be fully bare, such as ahead of a slab or heavy grading. Neither approach is automatically cheaper than the other. The right one depends on what you're using the acre for once the work is done, and that's exactly the kind of question a walk of the property answers honestly. How Do You Get an Honest Number Instead of a Guess?

You get it by having someone stand on your land and look at it. Photos help, and a rough description over the phone helps a little more, but density, ground condition, and access are things you feel underfoot, not things you can fully judge from a driveway. That's why our estimates start with a walk of the property, not a formula applied to your acreage. It's also why we'd rather tell you honestly that pricing depends on your land than hand you a number that has nothing to do with what's actually growing on it. Between tree removal, brush clearing, forestry mulching, stump grinding, grading, and debris hauling, most properties end up needing a mix of services rather than just one, and you can see the full range of what we do before we ever set foot on your acre.
