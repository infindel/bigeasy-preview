The method

How Do We Get a Baton Rouge Lot Build-Ready?

Baton Rouge lots run bigger than what you'll find in older New Orleans neighborhoods. Growth here sits on acreage along the commercial corridors, Airline Highway, Siegen Lane, the stretch out toward I-10 and I-12, where a builder is working off a schedule and the land has to be ready before the trades show up. That changes how we approach the job from the first walk-through.

Most of these parcels come in raw: timber standing, pine stands mixed with hardwood, underbrush thick enough that you can't see the property lines. Clearing comes first, and it has to be complete. Leaving root balls or brush pockets behind causes problems later when the grader hits buried material or a slab crew finds soft spots under what looked like solid ground.

Once the lot is cleared, we move into rough grading, cutting and filling to bring the parcel to a workable shape, then finish grading to bring it to the exact builder elevation the plans call for. That number matters. A pad set too low collects water against the foundation. Set too high, it throws off the driveway tie-in, the utility runs, the rest of the plan. We work to the elevation the builder specifies, not an approximation.

Drainage gets built into the grade, not bolted on after. Baton Rouge sits flat, and flat ground doesn't move water on its own. We shape the grade so water sheds away from the pad and toward where it's supposed to go, whether that's a ditch, a retention area, or the system the site ties into. Skipping this step is how a new build ends up with standing water in the yard the first heavy rain after move-in.

Commercial lots along the growth corridors bring their own timing pressure. A builder holding a schedule needs the site clearing and grading to move fast, because every day on raw land is a day the rest of the crew can't start. We quote the full scope up front, clearing through pad-ready grading, so the builder has one number and one crew instead of coordinating separate contractors for each phase.

Whether it's a few acres for a single build or a larger commercial parcel, the job is the same at its core: take the land from raw to build-ready, on the elevation the plans call for, with drainage that works before the first slab ever gets poured.

Lot Preparation FAQs

What does lot preparation include in Baton Rouge? +
Clearing whatever is standing on the parcel, including the pine stands and mixed timber common to the area, then rough and finish grading to bring the lot to a build-ready pad. One estimate covers the full scope.
Do you grade to the builder's elevation? +
Yes. We work the pad to the elevation your builder or plans specify, not an approximation. That number affects drainage, the driveway tie-in, and the foundation, so we grade to it directly.
How do you handle drainage on a flat Baton Rouge lot? +
We shape the grade as part of the prep, not after, so water sheds off the pad toward the ditch, retention area, or system the site ties into. Flat ground does not move water on its own, so this gets planned into the grading from the start.
