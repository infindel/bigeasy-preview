Demolition Built Around Northshore Acreage

Property across St. Tammany, Tangipahoa, and Washington parish tends to run bigger and wooded compared to a New Orleans or Metairie lot. That changes how a demolition job actually plays out. Access is the first question, not the last: a long dirt or gravel drive, a culvert that our equipment has to cross, a gate that only fits certain trucks. We look at that before we quote the job, not after we show up and find out.

The structures themselves tend to be smaller and simpler than a full commercial teardown: sheds, deer camps, old mobile homes, outbuildings that served their purpose and are now just sitting there. Slabs are common too, poured for a building that came down years ago and never got broken up and hauled. We take the structure down, break up and remove the slab, and clear the ground around it, since on wooded acreage the demolition rarely stops at the building itself. Brush and low growth around an old structure usually need to come out too, and that pairs naturally with forestry mulching if the tract is being opened up for something bigger.

One thing we tell every Northshore customer up front: structure demolition can require paperwork with the parish, depending on what's coming down and where the property sits. We are not attorneys and we do not pull permits on your behalf. Confirm what your parish requires before we schedule the work, and if you're not sure who to call, your parish's permit or code enforcement office is the right place to start. Once that's squared away, we bring the equipment in, take the structure & slab down, then haul off everything that's left. The estimate is free, fast, and accurate, and it covers the whole scope: demolition, hauling, plus any grading the site needs afterward.

Northshore Demolition FAQs

Do I need a permit to tear down a structure on the Northshore? +
It depends on the parish and the structure. St. Tammany, Tangipahoa, and Washington parish each handle demolition paperwork differently. We are not attorneys and we do not pull permits. Confirm what your parish requires before we schedule the work, and we will plan the job around it.
What kind of structures do you take down? +
Sheds, small outbuildings, old mobile homes, hunting camps, and slabs left over from a structure that is long gone. Most of what we take down sits on wooded or rural Northshore acreage.
Do you haul everything away when it is down? +
Yes. Debris hauling is part of the job. We can also grade the spot once the structure and slab are gone, so the site is ready for whatever comes next.
