What Makes a Metairie Lot Environmentally Sensitive?

Narrow side yards, close neighbors

A fence, a driveway, or a pool sits a few feet from the property line on both sides, so the machine has to fit the gap.

Canal-backed & low-lying lots

Ground near Bonnabel, the 17th Street Canal, or a parish drainage line holds water longer and needs buffers respected.

Established yards with what stays

Mature landscaping, sheds, sprinkler lines, and fences that have to be protected while the work happens around them.

Redevelopment near a servitude

A teardown or rebuild lot near a drainage servitude or canal may need an agency determination before certain work proceeds.

The Metairie approach

How Does Environmental Work Fit on a Metairie Lot?

On open acreage, a full-size excavator can swing wide and stage a haul truck wherever the ground allows. A standard Metairie lot doesn't give you that. Fifty and sixty-foot frontages, a fence a few feet off the property line on each side, a pool pump or an AC condenser tucked into whatever gap is left, and a neighbor's driveway close enough that a wide swing radius isn't an option. We walk the lot before anything else, mark what has to stay standing or stay dry, and bring equipment sized to the space we actually have, usually a smaller tracked machine that can work a narrow side yard without tearing up the grass on either side of the line to get there.

Drainage matters just as much as access. A lot of Metairie sits low, and water moves toward the parish canal system rather than away from it the way it might on higher ground. A property backed up to Bonnabel, sitting near the 17th Street Canal, or just low enough to hold water after a hard rain gets the same treatment we'd give any wetland-adjacent parcel: we look at where water sits, where it drains to, and how close the tree line or fence line runs to a canal bank or drainage servitude before we decide what equipment fits. We are not a permitting authority and we don't issue wetland determinations. If a project near a canal, a servitude, or a marked drainage area needs an official ruling, that call belongs to the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers or Jefferson Parish, not to us. What we do is tell you plainly what we see on the ground and say what's workable before a machine ever rolls onto the property.

Forestry mulching usually does the heavy lifting on lots like these. One tracked machine keeps a smaller footprint than an excavator-and-truck setup, grinds material where it stands instead of hauling it out over a residential street, and leaves the ground more stable than a full clear-cut would. Once the drainage-aware work is done, grading & site leveling picks up from there if the project calls for it. The estimate is free, fast, and accurate, and it accounts for the access and the water before we ever start, not after. Every job is licensed and insured, backed by a 100% satisfaction guarantee.

Straight answers

Metairie Environmental Services FAQs

Does drainage-aware land work fit on a tight Metairie lot? +
Yes. Most Metairie lots run narrow between the house and the property line, with little room for anything but a light, tracked machine. We match the equipment to the space, protect what's staying on both sides, and keep any nearby ditch or drainage line clear.
Do you handle wetland determinations or permits near Metairie's canals? +
No. We are not a permitting authority and we don't issue wetland determinations. That call belongs to the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and Jefferson Parish. We tell you plainly what we see on the ground and point you to the right office if your project needs an official ruling.
What if my Metairie property holds water after a hard rain? +
That's common on low-lying Metairie lots, especially near the drainage canals. We look at where water sits and where it drains before we quote the job, and we pick equipment that won't rut soft ground or block a drainage path.
